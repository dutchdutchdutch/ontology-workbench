# Prose style

How to write anything a human reads in this workspace: reports, definitions, queue items, cover
sheets. One page.

The point: the readers are product people, engineers, designers and domain experts, most of them not
writing in their first language, reading in a hurry.

---

## Rules

- **Conclusions first.** The finding, then the evidence, then the reasoning. A reader who stops
  after one sentence should have the answer.
- **No em dashes.** Use a comma, a full stop, or a colon.
- **Plain words for an international audience.** Short words. One meaning per word, one word per
  thing. If a technical term is unavoidable, define it the first time.
- **Short sentences.** About 20 words. One idea each. Active voice.
- **Tables for comparisons.** Anything that compares two or more things across two or more
  properties is a table, not a paragraph.
- **Verbatim quotes stay verbatim.** Never tidy a quote. The mess is the evidence.
- **Say when something is empty.** "No prior glossary existed, so this section is empty by design"
  beats a missing heading. A reader cannot tell a deliberate blank from a forgotten one.
- **Name the file, not the step.** "See `03-merge/conflicts.md`" tells the reader where to click.
  "See the merge step" does not.

## Definitions

A definition says what the thing is, then what it is for, then how to tell it from its nearest
neighbour. Test it by swapping the term for its closest synonym: if the sentence still reads true,
the definition is too loose.

Do not define a term with itself, and do not define it with a term that is itself undefined.
